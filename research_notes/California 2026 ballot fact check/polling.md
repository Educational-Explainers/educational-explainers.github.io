# California November 2026 ballot explainer — polling fact check

Snapshot date used by the page and by this check: October 5, 2026. Page under review: `ca-2026-ballot/index.html`. Cards store `poll` as `[yes, no, note]`. When both yes and no are numbers, the page prints `undecided/other` as `Math.max(0, 100 - yes - no)` (`pollMarkup` in that file). This check compares those figures to the original releases, not to news rewrites, except where a later poll is noted as a newer snapshot.

Verdict scale used below: ACCURATE, MOSTLY ACCURATE, INACCURATE, MISLEADING, UNVERIFIED. A card is ACCURATE when the printed yes, no, pollster, and month match the original likely-voter topline. A one-point gap between the code’s residual and the poll’s published don’t-know/undecided is recorded on the card but does not by itself change an otherwise exact yes/no citation. Prop 41 and Prop 43 are judged on the notes, because those notes make factual claims beyond the percentages.

### Inferences
- Wrong-pollster errors: none. Where the page chooses Berkeley (3, 39, 40), the percentages match Berkeley and do not match PPIC. Where it chooses PPIC, the percentages match PPIC.
- Wrong-population errors: none. Cards are likely voters. PPIC’s adult margin (±3.0, n = 1,745) and registered-voter margin (±3.3, n = 1,475) are not the proposition base.
- Outdated-wave errors inside the cited series: the PPIC numbers are that survey’s only September wave, not an earlier PPIC month. The Berkeley numbers are Release #2026-20 (September 25), not the August wave (Prop 40 was 48/41/11 and Prop 39 was 42/51/7 in that earlier Berkeley wave). The page is still an older snapshot as of October 5 for Props 39 and 40, because IVP (and the September 21–24 survey reported by NBC) came out after Berkeley and before October 5. For Props 4, 41, 42, and 43, the page uses PPIC even though Berkeley’s September 25 release already had different likely-voter numbers.
- Prop 41 is not a case of a published yes and a withheld no. Both shares were in the public PPIC write-up. Leaving no blank understates a 51–44 result with 5 percent volunteered don’t know.
- Prop 43 is not a case of PPIC refusing a number. The exact topline was in the same “Questions and Responses” block as the other propositions. “Below majority” alone would have been fair for the yes side. Pairing it with “exact topline unpublished” is not.
- PPIC yes + no + don’t know misses 100 only by rounding, on Props 5 (99), 37 (99), 39 (99), 42 (101), and 44 (101). There is no fourth substantive category in those toplines. Berkeley misses 100 only on Prop 3 (101). The page residual therefore disagrees with the published undecided/don’t-know by 1 point on Props 3 (12 vs 13), 5 (6 vs 5), 37 (4 vs 3), 42 (3 vs 4), and 44 (5 vs 6). It matches on Props 1, 2, 4, 38, 39, 40, and 45. Props 41 and 43 print no residual.
- PPIC don’t-know is volunteered and small (1 to 6 percent). Berkeley undecided is an offered category and much larger (9 to 30 percent on the seven measures). The page’s label “undecided/other” fits Berkeley’s offered undecided more naturally than PPIC’s volunteered don’t know, but the arithmetic remainder is the same bucket whenever the three published figures sum to 100.
