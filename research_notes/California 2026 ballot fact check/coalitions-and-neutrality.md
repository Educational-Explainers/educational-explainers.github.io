# California November 2026 ballot: coalition labels, partisan colors, and neutrality

Scope: the lean labels and color classes in `ca-2026-ballot/index.html`, plus specified factual premises in titles, one-liners, type tags, cost lines, and pro/con bullets. Record date is October 5, 2026. The official voter guide is already posted, and county officials begin mailing ballots on or before this date. Party positions below are from the UC Berkeley Institute of Governmental Studies endorsement table checked September 28, 2026, unless a voter-guide signer list is more specific. The IGS row for the California Republican Party links to `cagop.org/prop50/`, an odd URL; treat that row as a library compilation, not a page this review opened on cagop.org. Where NBC 7 or the voter-guide signers agree, that is noted.

Color classes in the page code: dem = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 37, 40; rep = 39, 41, 42, 43, 45; none = 38, 44.

Verdict scale: ACCURATE, MOSTLY ACCURATE, MISLEADING, INACCURATE, or UNVERIFIED. A label is MISLEADING if it assigns a party color when the visible coalition is mixed, or if it names one side and drops an equally important part of the other.

## Does each lean label match the filed supporters and opponents?

### Takeaway
Most labels track the voter-guide signers and the September 28 IGS party/union/business grid. The clear problems are the Democratic color on Proposition 40, the Republican color on Proposition 43, and the phrase “election-integrity opposition” on Proposition 5. Propositions 38 and 44 are correctly left uncolored. Proposition 45’s Republican color matches a California Republican Party Yes / California Democratic Party No split even though the visible text describes a business-versus-environment fight.

### Inferences
- Democratic color is justified for 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5 because the state Democratic Party is Yes, the state Republican Party is No or neutral, and the voter-guide signers match that split. Proposition 1 is the soft case: the Republican Party is neutral and no No argument was filed, so “conservative opposition” means HJTA and some Republican legislators, not a party campaign.
- Proposition 37’s dem color is defensible only as a description of the support side. The text already says the opposition is cross-ideological. That combination is mostly accurate, not a hidden party paint job.
- Proposition 40 is the opposite case. A dem color next to a label that leads with the Governor’s opposition tells the reader the card is Democratic while the Democratic governor and a large set of Democratic-aligned institutions are the opposition. The California Democratic Party’s Yes endorsement makes the picture a split, not a Democratic campaign.
- Proposition 43’s red color describes who is campaigning now, not who put it on the ballot. The text tries to hold both facts. The color throws one away.
- Proposition 45’s red color is the one business-versus-environment color that the state parties actually match. It should not be grouped with 43.
- “Election-integrity” on Proposition 5 should be replaced with the signers’ offices. It is the only lean phrase that adopts a slogan the official argument does not use.

### Gaps
- Cal-Access / FPPC dollar rankings for each committee were not pulled. “Major committee” here means voter-guide signers, the official quick-reference supporter line, and the IGS grid, not a contribution ranking.
- The CAGOP endorsement page was not opened directly. The IGS link for that row points at a Prop 50 URL.
- Party breakdowns of the SB 417, SB 42, and ACA 22 floor votes were not retrieved, beyond the aye/no totals and, for SB 42, a secondary report that the vote was party-line.
- Proposition 42’s Yes signers and Proposition 44’s Yes signers were not copied in full.
- No Democratic legislative-leadership endorsement or opposition on Proposition 40, separate from the state party and the Governor, was found.
- Whether Proposition 38 has a funded No committee is unverified. The voter guide lists one individual opponent.

## Are the specified factual premises true, and is neutral copy loaded?

### Takeaway
The housing-unit, earmark, voter-ID, audit-conflict, five-year penalty recovery, and tribal-consultation premises match the Legislative Analyst or the Attorney General summary. The Proposition 40 “bars replacement” bullet overstates a contested clause. “Poison pill,” “election-integrity,” and the type-tag word “countermeasure” are loaded. Titles, one-liners, and cost lines that were checked against the official fiscal summaries were not false. “Without evidence of widespread voter fraud” is an argument, not an LAO finding, and this review did not find an official 2026 finding that either proves or disproves it.

**Proposition 4 pro: bars education, transportation, and public-safety earmarks. Con: no overall cap.**
- The Attorney General summary: “Prohibits public funding programs from using funds earmarked for education, transportation, or public safety.” Government Code section 85300(a), as amended by SB 42, uses that same list. The LAO analysis repeats it. — [Title and summary](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/propositions/4/title-summary.htm); [SB 42 text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB42); [Analysis](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/propositions/4/analysis.htm)
- The measure does not create a program and does not set a dollar cap on future programs. It leaves design to later state and local action, with eligibility and spending-limit rules but no overall appropriation cap. CalTax makes that point, and the LAO says future program costs are not set by this measure. The con bullet is fair. The FPPC cost line, a few hundred thousand dollars a year, matches the fiscal summary. — [Analysis](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/propositions/4/analysis.htm); [CalTax](https://caltax.org/prop4/)

**Proposition 5 “election-integrity opposition.”**
- See the signer finding above. The phrase is a frame, not the opponents’ name. Jones and Patterson argue loss of the replacement vote. — [Arguments](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/propositions/5/arguments-rebuttals.htm)

**Proposition 38: half to one institute; price discount; admin cap; “limited organized opposition.”**
- Attorney General summary: $8.4 billion, allocated equally between one UC-affiliated nonprofit institute and research grants; treatments sold in California at 20 percent below the national average price; General Fund gets 10 percent of licensing royalties until the bonds are repaid. Fiscal impact: $500 million to $600 million a year for about 20 years. — [Title and summary](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/propositions/38/title-summary.htm)
- LAO: at least $4.2 billion for cancer, heart disease, and Alzheimer’s; no more than 2 percent for state administration; about half the funds to one institute selected by the California Department of Public Health; a 20 percent California patient discount. — [LAO](https://lao.ca.gov/BallotAnalysis/Proposition?number=38&year=2026); [IGS Prop 38](https://igs.berkeley.edu/library/california-ballot-proposition-guides/november-3-2026-general-election/proposition-38)
- The page’s con bullet “Half of the money is directed to one selected institute” matches the official summary. The pro bullet’s “admin cap,” “revenue sharing,” and “California price discount” match the 2 percent cap, the 10 percent royalty return, and the 20 percent discount, without stating those numbers. The cost line matches the official 20-year, $500–600 million summary. A signed No argument cites roughly $500 million for 25 years; that figure conflicts with the printed fiscal summary, and the page follows the summary. — [Arguments](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/propositions/38/arguments-rebuttals.htm)
- CalMatters reports the selection criteria are written so that they very likely fit one institute, the California Institute for Immunology and Immunotherapy at UCLA, whose donor backed the initiative. The page does not name that institute. “One selected institute” is still accurate. — [CalMatters](https://calmatters.org/politics/elections/2026/07/proposition-38-california/)

**Proposition 39: free state voter ID; citizenship-verification reporting; “without evidence of widespread voter fraud.”**
- LAO: the state must provide a voter identification card at no cost to voters who request one; officials must use “best efforts” and government data to verify citizenship and report every year the share of each county’s rolls that has been verified; the State Auditor reviews compliance in odd-numbered years. Cost: tens of millions to low hundreds of millions a year. — [LAO](https://lao.ca.gov/BallotAnalysis/Proposition?number=39&year=2026)
- The pro bullets on a free ID and citizenship-verification reporting and audits match that analysis. The page cost range matches it.
- The con bullet “without evidence of widespread voter fraud” is not a sentence in the LAO analysis. This review did not find a 2026 Secretary of State statistical finding that either documents or rules out widespread fraud. It is an opponent claim in an argument box, not a neutral-copy fact, and it is UNVERIFIED as a quantified institutional fact rather than shown false.

**Proposition 40: “after federal cuts”; “bars replacement of existing program funding”; dem color versus the Governor.**
- The measure text, as described by the Yes campaign and by the California Budget & Policy Center, says revenue shall not be used to supplant existing state funds for health care, education, or food assistance. — [Yes on 40 text guide](https://yeson40.com/the-initiative/); [California Budget & Policy Center](https://calbudgetcenter.org/?issue=taxes-revenue); [Mercury News](https://www.mercurynews.com/?p=13070686/)
- A CalMatters memo says proposed section 16355(d) has that non-supplantation sentence, while 16355(b)(1) allows 90 percent of the money to “restore or address any reductions in federal funding or state appropriations.” The memo calls that a loophole. An LA Times opinion piece makes the same criticism without quoting the subdivision. — [CalMatters memo PDF](https://calmatters.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Memo_Health-Care-Funding-Under-Prop-40_9.18.26.pdf); [LA Times opinion](https://www.latimes.com/opinion/story/2026-09-29/proposition-40-spending-safeguards)
- The pro bullet “bars replacement of existing program funding” states the non-supplantation clause as an unqualified bar. That is the proponents’ reading. It is not a settled institutional fact, because the same section is publicly described as also allowing the money to address reductions in state appropriations.
- “After federal cuts” is the sponsors’ rationale. SEIU-UHW, the official Yes argument, and a September 2026 explainer by the measure’s economists describe the tax as a response to federal health, education, and nutrition funding losses. The LAO fiscal summary cited in news is “tens of billions” over several years, with a possible ongoing income-tax loss under $1 billion a year, which is what the page’s cost line says. The LAO summary does not, in the sources reviewed, certify a specific federal-cut dollar figure. — [Arguments](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/propositions/40/arguments-rebuttals.htm); [Saez explainer PDF](https://eml.berkeley.edu/~saez/prop40explainer-sept2026.pdf); [Mercury News](https://www.mercurynews.com/?p=13070686/)
- Newsom is a Democrat and opposes the measure. The California Democratic Party endorses it. See the Proposition 40 coalition finding. The dem color is the neutrality problem. The lean sentence itself does not call the measure Democratic.

**Proposition 41 con: “Functions as a poison pill against Prop 40.” Type tag: “countermeasure to Prop 40.”**
- “Poison pill” does not appear in the official title. It is loaded tone inside the con box.
- The underlying design is real. The official summary says Proposition 41 nullifies state taxes enacted after January 1, 2026, that exempt revenue from the state spending limit. Proposition 40 exempts its revenue from that limit. If both pass, the one with more Yes votes controls, and the LAO-based explainers say 41 can keep 40 from taking effect. — [Quick reference](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/quick-reference-guide/41.htm); [California Budget & Policy Center](https://calbudgetcenter.org/?issue=taxes-revenue); [Pasadena Now](https://pasadenanow.com/main/guest-opinion-suzanne-york-the-billionaire-tax-has-two-trojan-horses-on-the-ballot)
- The type tag sits outside the pro/con boxes. “Countermeasure” states a purpose. The official subject is audits and the spending limit. The conflict is real; the word is argumentative in a neutral slot. SEIU’s voter-guide argument uses the same purpose claim without the words “poison pill.” — [Arguments](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/propositions/41/arguments-rebuttals.htm)

**Proposition 42 con: “Its immediate function is to nullify Prop 40 if it wins more Yes votes.” Type tag: “countermeasure to Prop 40.”**
- SEIU’s official No argument says 42 is “designed specifically to undo” Proposition 40. Budget & Policy Center says 42 conflicts because the wealth tax reaches personal property and looks back to residence on January 1, 2026, and that a higher Yes vote can invalidate 40. — [Arguments](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/propositions/42/arguments-rebuttals.htm); [California Budget & Policy Center](https://calbudgetcenter.org/?issue=taxes-revenue)
- The “if it wins more Yes votes” clause is the correct conflict rule. “Immediate function” is purpose language. Proposition 42 also bans future state personal-property taxes and certain retroactive taxes even if Proposition 40 fails, so nullifying 40 is not its only legal effect. Not a false conflict description; an overstated purpose. The same “countermeasure” type-tag issue applies.

**Proposition 43 referral.**
- Covered above. Democratic author, 68–2 and 35–1, author now opposes, campaign is taxpayer groups versus labor. Red color is the problem. No loaded word in the title “Two-thirds vote for local special taxes” was found; that title is plainer than the official title “Limits Voters’ Ability to Raise Revenues for Local Government Services.”

**Proposition 44 pro: “a five-year cure period.”**
- LAO: a clinic that misses the 90 percent standard pays a penalty equal to the shortfall and can get that money back if it complies within five years. If it does not, the state keeps the money for clinic workforce programs. Waivers exist. — [Voter-guide analysis](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/propositions/44/analysis.htm); [IGS](https://igs.berkeley.edu/library/california-ballot-proposition-guides/november-3-2026-general-election/proposition-44)
- “Five-year cure period” is a fair shorthand for penalty recovery, not a five-year delay before the rule starts. Not a false institutional fact.

**Proposition 45 con: “narrows judicial and tribal review.”**
- LAO: for eligible projects that opt in, consultation is required only with California Native American tribes formally recognized by the federal government, whereas current law requires consultation with a wider set of tribes. The analysis also narrows what courts review and limits remedies. — [LAO](https://lao.ca.gov/BallotAnalysis/Proposition?number=45&year=2026)
- Environmental Science Associates describes the same narrowing, from Native American Heritage Commission-listed non-federally recognized tribes to federally recognized tribes, and notes there is no deadline that cuts consultation short. “Narrows tribal review” matches the LAO. It does not abolish tribal consultation. — [ESA](https://esassoc.com/news-and-ideas/2026/09/proposition-45-explained-how-californias-ballot-measure-could-reshape-ceqa/)

**Neutral-copy scan (title, one-liner, type, cost), distinct from pro/con boxes.**
- No false dollar figure was found in the cost lines checked against the printed fiscal summaries for Props 1, 4, 38, and 39.
- Loaded or purposive wording outside the argument boxes: Proposition 41 and 42 type tags, “countermeasure to Prop 40.” Proposition 40’s dem color, which is a label rather than a sentence. Proposition 5’s “election-integrity,” which is inside the lean label, not the pro/con box.
- “Poison pill” is inside the con box. Tone, not a neutral-copy defect, and not an empty claim.

### Inferences
- The pro/con boxes are mostly faithful compressions of the LAO and the official arguments. The one institutional overclaim is Proposition 40’s unqualified “bars replacement” sentence.
- “Without evidence of widespread voter fraud” should stay in the con box if it stays at all, and it should not be restated as a finding. It was not shown to be a fabricated statistic because no number is attached, and it was not confirmed by the LAO.
- The page’s Proposition 38 cost line correctly prefers the official 20-year fiscal summary over the opponent’s 25-year figure.

### Gaps
- The full statutory text of Proposition 40 section 16355 was not opened on the Secretary of State PDF in this pass. The non-supplantation conflict rests on the Yes campaign’s quotation, the Budget Center, Mercury News, and a CalMatters memo quoting subdivisions (d) and (b)(1).
- No 2026 Secretary of State or LAO table of voter-fraud prosecutions was reviewed, so the Proposition 39 fraud negative stays UNVERIFIED rather than INACCURATE.
- Committee expenditure totals that would prove “limited organized opposition” to Proposition 38 were not pulled from Cal-Access.

## Are the glossary definitions false?

### Takeaway
None of the short definitions is false. The Gann Limit line is a correct short description of Article XIII B. A few entries are simplified, not wrong.

### Cited Findings
- The page defines LAO as the California Legislative Analyst’s Office, the nonpartisan fiscal adviser to the Legislature that writes ballot fiscal analyses. That is the office that signed the analyses cited throughout this note. — page glossary in `ca-2026-ballot/index.html`; analyses at [lao.ca.gov](https://lao.ca.gov/BallotAnalysis/Proposition?number=1&year=2026)
- PPIC as the Public Policy Institute of California, and Berkeley IGS as the Institute of Governmental Studies at UC Berkeley, match the names of the polling and endorsement sources the page and this note use. — [IGS](https://igs.berkeley.edu/library/california-ballot-proposition-guides/november-3-2026-general-election/november-3-2026)
- FPPC as the Fair Political Practices Commission, the campaign-finance and ethics agency, matches the agency named in the Proposition 4 fiscal summary. — [Prop 4 title and summary](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/propositions/4/title-summary.htm)
- CEQA as the California Environmental Quality Act, the state process for reviewing environmental effects, matches how the Proposition 45 official title uses the statute. Calling it a “process” omits that it is a statute, but it is not a wrong definition. — [Prop 45 quick reference](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/quick-reference-guide/45.htm)
- CalHFA as the California Housing Finance Agency matches the agency named in the Proposition 37 Yes argument. — [Arguments](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/propositions/37/arguments-rebuttals.htm)
- CalVet as the California Department of Veterans Affairs, which runs the veteran home-loan program, matches the Proposition 1 argument’s description of the CalVet Home Loan Program. — [Arguments](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/propositions/1/arguments-rebuttals.htm)
- SEIU as the Service Employees International Union matches the union in “SEIU-United Healthcare Workers West,” the Proposition 40 and 41/42 signer. — [Prop 40 arguments](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/propositions/40/arguments-rebuttals.htm)
- General Fund as the main operating account supported largely by personal income, sales, and corporation taxes is the standard description used in the Proposition 2 debate and is not contradicted by the fiscal summaries reviewed.
- Gann Limit: “The common name for California’s constitutional limit on certain state and local appropriations.” Proposition 41’s official title is “Prohibits New State Taxes That Exclude Revenues From State Spending Limit,” and the page’s own “yes” line for 41 calls that limit the Gann Limit. The 1979 Gann initiative is the constitutional appropriations limit on proceeds of taxes for the state and for local governments. “Certain” is the right hedge, because the limit does not cover every dollar the state spends. The definition is accurate, not merely harmless. — [SOS qualified measures](https://www.sos.ca.gov/elections/ballot-measures/qualified-ballot-measures); [Prop 41 quick reference](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/quick-reference-guide/41.htm)
- K–12 as kindergarten through 12th grade is correct. The page does not confuse it with the K–14 education share discussed in Proposition 40’s 10 percent account.

### Inferences
- No glossary correction is required for accuracy. If the page is edited for precision, the only useful addition on the Gann Limit would be that it limits appropriations of tax proceeds, not all spending. The current sentence does not need to be marked wrong.

### Gaps
- The glossary was checked against the ballot record and standard legal names, not against a separate statutory dictionary entry for every term. No conflict turned up.
