RECORD LABELS

What the tags mean

  • Has a recordThey acted on this in a past job: a vote, a law, a lawsuit, a program.
  • Changed positionWhat they say now is different from what they said or did before.
  • No record yetThey have not held a job where they could act on this.
  • Facts disputedReporters or the two sides disagree about what happened.
  • Not reviewed yetA record may exist, but we have not checked it yet.

Your local races are on your ballot too

Your ballot also has races that depend on where you live: U.S. House, State Senate, State Assembly, and county, city, and school board seats. This page covers local races for Santa Clara County so far. Pick your county in My candidate checklist to see them. For other counties, here is how to look them up:

  1. Open your county’s sample ballot. It came in the mail, or find it at your county elections office.
  2. Check your registration and districts at My Voter Status.
  3. Read candidate statements in the official state Voter Information Guide. For a lawmaker’s votes, search their name on California Legislative Information or Congress.gov.
  4. Use the same test as this page: what do they promise, and what have they actually done?

How this page was built

Who is on the ballot comes from the Secretary of State’s certified June 2 primary results (certified July 10, 2026) and the certified November candidate list. Plans come from each campaign’s website, the official voter guide, debates, and news reports. Records come from public votes, laws, court cases, official reports, and news fact-checks. Every record box links to its sources.

  • We give both candidates in a race the same kinds of facts.
  • Campaign claims are attributed. Some records remain unreviewed; a source link does not mean every claim has been independently verified.
  • Endorsement and fundraising summaries without direct supporting citations are withheld pending verification.
  • Polls are snapshots, not predictions. We list only polls from independent pollsters.

Words to know

Top-two primary
In June, all candidates run on one ballot. The top two go to November, even if they are from the same party.
Incumbent
The person who holds the job now.
Open seat
No incumbent is running, often because of term limits.
Endorsement
A public statement of support from a person or group.
Outside spending
Money spent by groups that are not the candidate’s own campaign.
Medi-Cal
California’s health coverage for people with low incomes.
Sanctuary law (SB 54)
A 2017 state law that limits how much local police help federal immigration agents.
CEQA
The state’s environmental review law. People can sue under it to slow or stop projects.
FAIR Plan
California’s last-resort home insurance for people who can’t get regular coverage.
FPPC
The state agency that enforces campaign money and ethics rules.
Cap-and-trade
A state program that makes big polluters pay for the carbon they release.
Low Carbon Fuel Standard
A state rule that pushes fuel makers to cut pollution from gas and diesel.

Sources

Use the links beside each issue for its evidence, or open a race below for its full reference list.

  • Election facts: certified candidate lists and results from the Secretary of State and county registrars.
  • Votes, laws and court decisions: official records first, with established reporting for context. A proposal, a passed bill and a law in effect are different things.
  • Promises and personal accounts: campaign statements, questionnaires and office press releases show what the candidate or office says. They are not independent proof of results.
  • Limits: unreviewed claims are marked. Endorsement and fundraising summaries without direct citations are withheld. This guide does not certify every statement in an external source.
