THE BALLOT AT A GLANCE

The fiscal questions are not all alike.

A bond is money the state borrows and pays back, with interest, over many years. Some bonds are paid back by the people who get loans, not the state. Some measures change what taxes can be created later, without moving any money today. Each card says which kind it is, so numbers that don’t belong together don’t get added up.

How this guide was built

Vote effects and fiscal estimates come from California's official Voter Information Guide and the nonpartisan Legislative Analyst's Office. Coalition labels summarize publicly named supporters and opponents. Pros and cons are the best good-faith cases made by each side, condensed for clarity. The research record shows the sources, the arithmetic, and which statements are facts and which are judgments. That check did not change this guide.

How to use the numbers

  • Annual cost is compared with the $251.477B enacted 2026–27 General Fund.
  • Authorization is not the same as annual cost.
  • Unknown means the LAO did not publish a defensible dollar estimate.
  • Per Californian divides a yearly cost by about 39.6 million residents (Department of Finance, Jan. 1, 2026). It is a way to picture the size, not a bill anyone receives.

This is a research snapshot, not an endorsement or a substitute for your county ballot instructions. Polling and campaign coalitions may change after October 5, 2026.

READING KEY

Glossary & references

Decode the short forms used throughout the guide, then open the primary budget and polling sources.

Agencies, laws, and budget terms

Bond
Money the state borrows from investors and pays back, with interest, over many years.
General-obligation bond
A bond the state promises to repay, usually from its main budget (the General Fund).
Revenue bond
A bond repaid only by a specific source of money, such as borrowers’ loan payments, not by the state budget.
Debt service
The yearly payments the state makes to pay back a bond, including interest.
Net worth
Everything a person owns minus everything they owe.
Special tax
A tax whose money must be used for a set purpose, such as parks or fire protection.
Spending cap (Gann Limit)
A limit in the state constitution on how much the state and local governments can spend each year.
Rainy-day fund
State savings set aside for bad years, when tax money drops.
Recall
A special vote on whether to remove an elected official before their term ends.
LAO
California Legislative Analyst’s Office—the nonpartisan fiscal and policy adviser to the Legislature and the author of ballot-measure fiscal analyses.
PPIC
Public Policy Institute of California, whose Statewide Survey supplies many of the polling snapshots.
Berkeley IGS
Institute of Governmental Studies at UC Berkeley, which conducts the Berkeley IGS Poll.
FPPC
California Fair Political Practices Commission, the state agency for campaign-finance and government-ethics rules.
CEQA
California Environmental Quality Act, the state process for reviewing a project’s environmental effects.
CalHFA
California Housing Finance Agency, the state’s housing-finance agency.
CalVet
California Department of Veterans Affairs, which runs the state’s veteran home-loan program.
SEIU
Service Employees International Union, a labor organization named in some campaign coalitions.
GF / General Fund
The state’s main operating account, supported largely by personal income, sales, and corporation taxes.
K–12
Public education from kindergarten through 12th grade.
